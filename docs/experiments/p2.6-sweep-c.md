# Phase 2 sweep C: the largest models on T1 and T2 (P2.6)
- status: done
- result: **positive (descriptive)**. Both models have a non-zero T2
  application worst-case gap (95% CIs exclude 0): Llama-3.3-70B W8A8
  5.7 pp (JSON → owner) and gpt-oss-120b 1.2 pp (NL → Cedar). Llama's T1 gap
  on the same rows is 5.5 pp, with 97.7% row agreement, so its gap is a
  decision effect, not a format failure. NL has the highest deny→allow rate
  for both. The optional models were not run. Holm inference and the scaling
  test wait for P2.9.

## Question / hypothesis

This report answers Q1–Q3 of `docs/prereg/phase2.md`, without any directional
prediction, for the two largest sweep models:
- **Q1.** Does application accuracy depend on the rendering? (worst-case gap)
- **Q2.** What is the error direction?
- **Q3.** Is the interpretation/application dissociation rendering-dependent?

Q4 (scaling) needs the whole sweep (P2.9).

## Method

- **Data.** The frozen Phase-1 benchmark v2: 1,984 worlds (544 real, 1,440
  synthetic), six renderings, 172,296 rows (95,232 application). Dataset
  sha256 `0b602a1701bc52aaaf676973f24ea87b1e27acd9029f5e0523174d51894a4f83`,
  re-checked on the H100 after staging.
- **Config.** `configs/authinv/t2.yaml`, seed 20261010, temperature 0, parser
  `json-strict-v3` (generated under v3, so no re-score).
  - Llama-3.3-70B W8A8: `max_tokens` 256, `max_model_len` 8,192.
  - gpt-oss-120b: `max_tokens` 4,096, `max_model_len` 12,288. The answer is
    the harmony final channel.
- **Code and hardware.** Git `56b39c0` (`main`, after PR #48). vLLM 0.29.0,
  torch 2.13.0+cu130, transformers 5.16.1, one NVIDIA H100 PCIe (GPU 7, free
  for the whole sweep). Compose project `authinv-sweepc`.
- **GPU settings** (throughput only; decoding is greedy):
  - Llama T2: `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.98 --enforce-eager`. At 0.95 the
    engine fails start-up for lack of KV cache (`memory/reference-dgx-usage.md`).
  - Llama T1: `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.98`. The T1 runner has no
    `--enforce-eager` flag. The log shows 5 PyTorch allocator OOM warnings
    (0.8–1.1 GB requests); each was retried after freeing cache, and the run
    completed with exit 0.
  - gpt-oss-120b: `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.95`.
- **Tiers.**
  - **T2** (headline): greedy generation of all 172,296 rows.
  - **T1**: candidate log-probability margins on the 95,232 application rows,
    vLLM backend. The prereg's footnote (T1 only where the stack loads the
    checkpoint as published) is met: vLLM loads the W8A8 checkpoint natively.
  - T1 is not defined for gpt-oss (harmony output, prereg).
- **Statistics.** The world is the unit of analysis. CIs are world-clustered
  percentile bootstraps with 4,000 replicates. Accuracies are world means.
- **Wall time.** Llama T2 2 h 16 min, Llama T1 7 h 44 min, gpt-oss-120b T2
  3 h 5 min.

installed:

## Decision rule (pre-registered, `docs/prereg/phase2.md`, commit `91cf920`)

- The primary metric is the T2 application worst-case gap, reported with its
  CI and taken from the point estimates.
- No per-model pass/fail threshold was preregistered. Significance is judged
  on the Holm-adjusted rendering contrasts across all 12 sweep models (72
  tests, α = 0.05) in P2.9.
- **Quantization control.** Decided in P2.5 on Qwen3.8-27B bf16 vs FP8: the
  largest |Δ| (30.1 pp) is below the bf16 gap (45.7 pp), so the quantized
  configurations, including llama3_3_70b_int8, **stay in the primary table**.
  gpt-oss's MXFP4 is its native format, not a quantized configuration.
- Effect sizes come first.

## Result

**Q1.**
- **Llama-3.3-70B W8A8** has a 5.7 pp T2 gap (JSON 78.7 → owner 84.4). The
  T1 gap on the same rows is 5.5 pp, with the same worst and best renderings
  and 97.7% row agreement. Non-answers are ≤ 2.0% of application rows. Its
  gap is therefore a difference in decisions, not in answer format.
  - Overall application accuracy is low for a 70B model (79–84%): only 65.5%
    of instances are correct under all six renderings.
  - The gap is larger on synthetic worlds (7.3 pp) than on real ones
    (2.4 pp).
- **gpt-oss-120b** is near the ceiling: ≥ 98.7% on every rendering, 1.2 pp gap
  (NL is worst), 98.5% of instances correct under all six.
  - Next to gpt-oss-20b (P2.4), the application gap is about half (1.2 vs
    2.2 pp) and NL stays the worst rendering in both.

**Q2.**
- **Llama fails closed more often than open:** allow→deny 21.9–31.3% vs
  deny→allow 7.9–12.5% on T2, and the same pattern on T1. Instance-level:
  33.8% of instances have an allow→deny flip and 19.5% a deny→allow flip.
- **gpt-oss-120b fails open, almost only on NL:** NL deny→allow 2.5% vs
  ≤ 0.4% elsewhere, with allow→deny ≤ 0.1% everywhere.
- **NL has the highest deny→allow rate for both models,** on T2 and on
  Llama's T1. Together with sweeps A and B, NL is highest in 11 of the 12
  configurations run so far.

**Q3.**
- Llama's interpretation accuracy is low (48.8–59.6%; gap 10.8 pp, Rego
  worst). Its application error given correct interpretation is 8.0–9.0%,
  with little spread across renderings.
- gpt-oss-120b interprets at 97.1–98.8%, and wrong application after correct
  interpretation is ≤ 0.2%.

**Quantization.** No new control: under the P2.5 rule outcome, the W8A8
anchor stays in the primary table. There is no bf16 Llama-3.3-70B run to
compare it with, so the report makes no claim about what W8A8 does to it.

## Conclusion & implication

- **The rendering effect survives at the top of the sweep.** Both 70B and
  120B models have a non-zero gap. For Llama it is a decision-level effect
  (T1 ≈ T2), with almost no non-answers.
- **Reasoning models are near the ceiling, but NL still leaks.** gpt-oss-120b
  reaches ≥ 98.7% everywhere. Its residual errors are almost all fail-open on
  NL, as for gpt-oss-20b (NL deny→allow 4.5%), Gemma-4-31B and Qwen3.8 with
  thinking on. NL as the
  fail-open rendering is the most consistent pattern in the sweep so far.
- **Direction depends on the model.** Llama over-denies on every rendering,
  while the reasoning models over-allow on NL. P2.9 should report direction
  per model, not pooled.
- **Not claimed.** These are behavioral results (accuracy and likelihood
  margins); they say nothing about internal mechanisms. No Holm-adjusted
  significance or scaling claim is made until P2.9. The gpt-oss-20b/120b
  comparison and the Rego "accuracy if parsed" figure are descriptive.

# Phase 2 sweep A: models ≤14B on T1 and T2 (P2.4)
- status: done
- result: **positive (descriptive)**. All six models have a non-zero
  worst-case rendering gap on T2 application accuracy (2.2–7.3 pp; every 95% CI
  excludes 0). The formal inference (Holm across the 72-test family, and the
  scaling test) needs the full 12-model sweep and is made in P2.9.

## Question / hypothesis

This report answers Q1–Q3 of `docs/prereg/phase2.md`, without any directional
prediction, for the six sweep models of at most 14B parameters:
- **Q1.** Does application accuracy depend on the rendering? (worst-case gap)
- **Q2.** What is the error direction?
- **Q3.** Is the interpretation/application dissociation rendering-dependent?

Q4 (scaling) needs the whole sweep.

## Method

- **Data.** The frozen Phase-1 benchmark v2: 1,984 worlds (544 real, 1,440
  synthetic), six renderings, 172,296 rows (95,232 application). Dataset
  sha256 `0b602a1701bc52aaaf676973f24ea87b1e27acd9029f5e0523174d51894a4f83`.
- **Config.** `configs/authinv/t2.yaml` (config sha256 `bc2dd9d8…`),
  parser `json-strict-v2`, seed 20261010, temperature 0.
- **Code and hardware.** Every run used git `e586fae` (`main`), vLLM 0.29.0,
  torch 2.13.0+cu130, on one NVIDIA H100 PCIe (GPU 7).
- **GPU sharing.** The GPU was shared with another project's container, so
  every run used `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.5`. That changes throughput only:
  decoding is greedy.
- **Tiers.**
  - **T2** (headline): greedy generation of all 172,296 rows.
  - **T1**: candidate log-probability margins on the 95,232 application rows,
    using the vLLM backend (the prereg amendment).
  - gpt-oss-20b answers through harmony reasoning, so T1 is not defined for it
    (prereg).
- **Statistics.** The world is the unit of analysis. CIs are world-clustered
  percentile bootstraps with 4,000 replicates. A p of 0 means
  p < 1/4,000 before the factor of two.

Reproduce, on an H100 inside `vllm/vllm-openai:v0.29.0` with `cedarpy==4.12.1`
installed:

```sh
D=outputs/20261008T140619620402Z_authinv_benchmark
for m in qwen3_5_4b qwen3_5_9b gemma4_e4b gemma4_12b ministral3_14b gpt_oss_20b; do
  python3 scripts/authinv_eval.py run --config configs/authinv/t2.yaml --dataset $D --model $m \
    --gpu-memory-utilization 0.5
  [ "$m" = gpt_oss_20b ] || python3 scripts/authinv_t1_eval.py run --config configs/authinv/t2.yaml \
    --dataset $D --model $m --gpu-memory-utilization 0.5
done
```

## Decision rule (pre-registered, `docs/prereg/phase2.md`, commit `91cf920`)

- The primary metric is the T2 application worst-case gap, reported with its
  CI and taken from the point estimates.
- No per-model pass/fail threshold was preregistered. Significance is judged
  on the Holm-adjusted rendering contrasts across all 12 sweep models (72
  tests, α = 0.05) in P2.9.
- Effect sizes come first. This report gives the per-model raw contrasts as
  family members only.

## Result

**Positive (descriptive), pending the P2.9 family-wise inference.**

**Q1.** Every model's T2 worst-case gap is above zero, with CIs well clear of
zero: 4.3–7.3 pp for the five non-reasoning models and 2.2 pp for gpt-oss-20b.
Re-selecting best and worst inside each bootstrap replicate barely changes the
gaps, at most 0.2 pp. Between 2.9% and 32.5% of instances get different
answers under certified-equivalent renderings.

**Q1 pattern.** There's no single worst rendering across models.
- Rego is worst for Qwen3.5-4B/9B and Ministral-3-14B.
- NL is worst for Gemma-4-12B and gpt-oss-20b, and the table for Gemma-4-E4B.
- Rego is also *best* for Gemma-4-12B and gpt-oss-20b.

The worst rendering often differs between real and synthetic worlds for the
same model: it changes for four of the six (Qwen3.5-4B, Qwen3.5-9B,
Gemma-4-E4B and Ministral-3-14B).

**Q2.** The non-reasoning models mostly fail closed: allow→deny is 9–64%,
against deny→allow 3–26%.
- The fail-open (deny→allow) rate depends on the rendering in a consistent
  way. It is highest on the natural-language rendering for every model except
  Gemma-4-E4B, where the table is 0.2 pp higher.
- It is lowest on Rego in every model:
  - Qwen3.5-4B: NL 20.1% vs Rego 4.8%;
  - Qwen3.5-9B: 17.4% vs 5.3%;
  - Gemma-4-12B: 16.5% vs 6.9%;
  - Ministral-3-14B: 9.7% vs 3.1%;
  - gpt-oss-20b: 4.5% vs 0.1%.
- gpt-oss-20b's errors are almost entirely fail-open, on NL (4.5% vs ≤0.2%
  allow→deny).

So the most permissive misreadings come from the prose rendering, and
executable Rego is the most conservative.

**Q3.** Applying a policy wrongly despite interpreting it correctly happens 0–17%
of the time and varies across renderings by up to 7 pp within a model
(Ministral-3-14B: Cedar 2.9% vs JSON 10.0%). Interpretation accuracy itself is
low (31–69%) for every model except gpt-oss-20b (93–98%).

**T1 vs T2.** On the four models with matched candidate tokens, T1 reproduces
T2 closely: the same worst and best renderings in three of four models, gaps
within 0.3 pp, and 96.5–99.5% row agreement. The effect is present in the
models' answer likelihoods, not only in their sampled text.

## Conclusion & implication

- **Engine certification does not make renderings equivalent for these
  models.** Certified-equivalent renderings of the same authorization policy
  produce measurably different decisions in all six models of at most 14B
  parameters, including the strongest of them (gpt-oss-20b, 2.2 pp, almost all
  of it fail-open on NL).
- **Accuracy rises, the gap doesn't shrink.** Across the dense models (66% →
  91% accuracy) the gap stays between 4 and 7 pp. That is consistent with Q4's
  test being worth running, but not evidence for it: scaling is tested once in
  P2.9.
- **No rendering is a safe default.** The worst rendering changes by model and
  by source kind, which is what the plan's worst-case framing is for.
- **Fail-open errors.** Prose renderings carry the largest deny→allow rates.
  T3 (P2.8) should test whether these turn into unauthorized actions.
- **Not claimed.** These are behavioral results (accuracy and likelihood
  margins). They say nothing about the models' internal mechanisms, and no
  Holm-adjusted significance or scaling claim is made until the sweep is
  complete.
